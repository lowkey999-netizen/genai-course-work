The Embedding Pipeline

1. Word → Vector

An embedding model turns a word into a list of numbers (a vector). These numbers aren't random. They are "sliders" that measure abstract concepts learned by the AI.

"loan" →
[ 0.65, -0.21, 0.88, ... 765 more ... ]

Under the hood (Metaphorical Sliders)

Note: Real dimensions are mathematically abstract, not clean human labels like "Finance".

2. The Embedding Space

If a vector has 3 numbers, we can plot it as a point in 3D space. Distance is meaning.

Watch how words naturally form clusters. The angle between them (Cosine Similarity) tells us how related they are.

Finance (Loan, Bank, Credit)
Medical (Doctor, Hospital, Nurse)
Sports (Cricket, Bat, Pitch)

3. The 768-Dimension Problem

Human brains can only visualize up to 3 dimensions (Up/Down, Left/Right, Forward/Back).

Nomic-embed-text outputs 768 dimensions.

We have a 768-dimensional cloud of points. We need to print it on a flat 2D screen to look at it.
How do we choose which 2 dimensions to keep?

4. PCA (Principal Component Analysis)

PCA asks: "If I shine a flashlight on this 3D cloud, what angle casts the widest, most spread-out shadow on the wall?"

It doesn't care about "Finance" or "Medical". It only looks for the axis where the data varies the most.

PC1: The longest spread of the data.
PC2: The next longest spread, perfectly perpendicular to PC1.
Rotating to find the best 2D projection...

5. SVD (Singular Value Decomposition)

PCA is the goal (find the best shadow). SVD is the engine that calculates it.

SVD proves that any crazy shape of data points can be broken down into exactly 3 geometric steps applied to a perfect circle:

  1. Vᵀ (Rotate): Align the grid.
  2. Σ (Stretch): Pull the axes. The amount of stretch are the "Singular Values" (the importance of each PC).
  3. U (Rotate): Spin it into final position.

SVD rips the 768D matrix apart to hand us the Principal Components on a silver platter.

6. The 2D Scatter Plot

We take our 768D points, multiply them by the top 2 directions PCA found, and we get this flat map.

WARNING: We threw away 766 dimensions!

Two points might look close together here on the screen, but could be wildly far apart in the hidden 766 dimensions. 2D is just a squashed shadow of the truth.
